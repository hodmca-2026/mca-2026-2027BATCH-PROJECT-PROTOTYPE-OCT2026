Employee Recognition Policy

Purpose
Employee recognition acknowledges contributions that support team goals, service quality, collaboration, innovation, safety, or other organizational priorities. Recognition may be informal or part of an approved formal program.

Recognition principles
Recognition should be timely, specific, respectful, and based on observable contributions rather than personal preference. Managers should explain what was achieved and why it mattered. Opportunities for recognition should be applied consistently across teams and should not discriminate against employees based on protected or irrelevant personal characteristics.

Forms of recognition
Depending on the approved program, recognition may include a thank-you message, team acknowledgement, certificate, development opportunity, or an approved award. Any monetary award or benefit must follow the relevant compensation and approval procedures; managers should not promise an unapproved reward.

Records and privacy
Formal recognition may be recorded using the organization's designated process. Public acknowledgement should respect employee preferences and confidentiality requirements. Recognition is not a substitute for fair performance evaluation, compensation review, or documented promotion criteria.

Responsibilities
Managers should use the approved program rules, and HR should communicate eligibility and administration details.

Questions
Employees should contact HR for the current recognition options and approval requirements.
