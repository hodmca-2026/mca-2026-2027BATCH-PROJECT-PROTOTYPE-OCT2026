Employee Goal-Setting Policy

Purpose
Goal setting connects an employee's day-to-day work with team priorities and organizational objectives. Goals should reflect the employee's role, level of responsibility, available resources, and agreed performance measures.

Creating goals
At the start of a review period, the employee and manager should discuss priorities and document the expected outcomes. Goals should be specific enough to guide action, measurable where practical, realistic, relevant to the role, and associated with a review date. The discussion should also identify dependencies, support needs, and how progress will be evidenced.

Reviewing goals
Goals should be revisited when responsibilities, business priorities, or available resources change materially. Updates should be documented rather than silently changing the standard against which work is assessed. Employees should raise blockers early and keep relevant progress records.

Responsibilities
Managers should explain priorities, give feedback, and avoid setting conflicting goals. Employees should ask questions when a target or success measure is unclear and share progress during agreed check-ins.

Use in performance reviews
Completed goals can inform performance discussions, but review decisions should also consider relevant role expectations and documented work evidence.

Questions
Employees should discuss goal changes or measurement concerns with their manager or HR.
